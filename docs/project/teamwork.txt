Team roles (5 people)
Person 1 — Simulation engine. Pure Python. Writes a script that generates realistic time-series for all 5 parameters using NumPy. Critically: it must inject synthetic anomaly events (sudden pH drop = pollution spike, temperature stratification = thermal layer shift). This is what makes the project original — you're not just reading sensors, you're modeling a real water body's behavior.
Person 2 — AI models. Trains an Isolation Forest on the clean simulated data to detect anomalies in real-time. Trains a simple LSTM (Keras/TensorFlow) to produce a 24-hour forecast of each parameter. This is what scores the AI bonus criterion.
Person 3 — Backend. FastAPI app that subscribes to the MQTT stream from Person 1, runs the models from Person 2, and exposes clean REST endpoints: /live, /forecast, /alerts.
Person 4 — 3D water scene. Three.js (or Plotly 3D as a simpler fallback). A rendered sea/river surface whose color and choppiness change in real-time based on incoming parameter values. When pH drops, the water turns slightly red. When dissolved O₂ is low, a warning pulse appears. This is the showstopper visual at the demo.
Person 5 — Dashboard + presentation. Assembles the full frontend: live Chart.js graphs for each parameter, an alert timeline panel, the 24h forecast chart, and the 3D scene. Also owns the demo narrative and slide deck.

Why this wins
Most teams will show a sensor feeding a number into a graph. Your system will show a living 3D water body that predicts the future and detects invisible pollution — standing next to actual seawater on Saint Anastasia Island. The jury will remember exactly one project.
